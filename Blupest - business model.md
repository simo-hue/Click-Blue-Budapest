# Blupest: Product Concept and Business Model (EIT Water CLICK BLUE, 2026)

> **Working name:** Blupest · **Draft tagline:** TBD · **Team:** Saeed Fathallah Khanloobrise, Simone Mattioli, Andreas Herzinger

**One-liner:** Blupest turns a neglected stretch of the Buda Danube bank into a green, clean, quieter place where people gather, play sport and learn, because people take care of a river they enjoy being around.

**Legal form:** non-profit, run by a community club. Funded mainly by sponsors and the community, with possible support from Budapest / Hungary.

Status legend: **[src]** = sourced · **[est]** = our estimate · **[TBC]** = to confirm

**Pilot site:** Buda bank, Műegyetem rakpart, between Szabadság and Petőfi bridges: two existing pontoons near BME and the stone bank between them. This is a proof of concept: the model works on any Budapest pontoon, so if this one isn't available we move to another one, including unused pontoons. Pontoon owners who don't host us can still join as community contributors.

## 1. Problem

**The big picture:** the Danube carries an estimated **4.2 tonnes of plastic into the Black Sea every day** (about 1,533 t a year). In the Austrian Danube, drifting plastic even outnumbered fish larvae. [src: Lechner et al. 2014, Environmental Pollution; data 2010–2012]

**Where it starts: trash on the banks.** Litter left on the bank by people, or dropped there by high water, goes back into the river. Ship waves pick up plastic lying near the waterline [src: Climo et al. 2022, Frontiers in Water], and a 10-year flood multiplies the plastic a river picks up tenfold [src: Roebroek et al. 2021, Environmental Research Letters]. From there it spreads downstream. Our bank, on one of the busiest Danube stretches, is covered with driftwood and trash (our photo, Nov 2026), and a CLEAR RIVERS litter trap sits on the pontoon in front of BME to catch plastic floating along the west bank. [src: petkupa.hu]

**Why it stays this way: a circle**

**Hard to reach → rarely used → little care → more litter → even less reason to come**

- **Hard to reach:** steep stone banks; a busy quay road where main-road noise above 68 dB (Lden) by day is typical [src: budapest.hu]; 27% of Budapest residents live with road noise above 65 dB [src: OVGU paper citing Budapest Environmental Assessment 2022]; and few toilets, with Budapest ranked 14th worst in Europe [src: dailynewshungary]. [Exact noise level at the site: to measure on site]
- **Rarely used:** the city centre offers very few public spots to sit, meet or play sport by the water. The Pest lower-quay renewal may finish by 2029 [src: bkk.hu], and plans for the Buda quay near Műegyetem are still being discussed [src: bkk.hu].
- **Little care:** a place people seldom visit has few people looking after it.
- **More litter:** trash builds up, and the place becomes even less inviting.

**The good news: people care more about places they enjoy.** A meta-analysis found that attachment to a place has a positive, moderate effect on pro-environmental behaviour, strongest for behaviour aimed at that same place. [src: Daryanto & Song 2021, Journal of Business Research]

**Blupest turns the circle around:** easy to reach → enjoyed → cared for → cleaner → more people come.

### Pains
1. Free, pleasant places by the water in the city centre are rare.
2. The bank is dirty and steep, with little seating, stairs or greenery.
3. Traffic noise makes the riverside unpleasant to stay at.
4. Toilets are far away, so visits stay short.
5. Kids grow up next to the Danube with little hands-on river awareness.

## 2. Solution

### How it works
We don't build from zero. We upgrade an **existing pontoon** and the bank next to it, then grow in three steps. A community club runs it with sports clubs and schools, so the place stays alive and cared for.

### Step 1: Activate one pontoon
- **Extend** the existing pontoon.
- **Green trash stopper:** a ring of planted hexagon modules (floating wetlands) around the extended pontoon catches floating litter, helped by a floating boom that guides litter into a catch basin (see "How the trash stopper works"). The hexagons are made from recycled plastic (see Technical approach).
- **Toilets:** accessible chemical toilets at street level, open to everyone.
- **Clean-up:** clean and open up a **20 m stretch of bank along the river, starting from the pontoon**.
- **Community club:** founded to run and maintain the site.
- **Sports partners:** sports clubs organise events at the site.
- **Schools:** river-awareness workshops.

### Step 2: Grow along the bank
- **Noise buffer:** a see-through line of plants and panels from sustainable materials along the street. It calms the space without walling it off like a private area.
- **Beach link:** extend the cleaned beach area **to the second pontoon**.
- **Second pontoon:** make it bigger and greener.
- **Public sport and health equipment on the pontoons:** easy-to-install, free equipment that anyone can use. One pontoon gets an outdoor calisthenics (bodyweight workout) area; the other gets other healthy, sporty uses, such as an open deck for yoga and stretching. Equipment is bolted onto the decks, so it can be added, moved or taken in for winter.

### Step 3: Modular platforms for people
- **Jetfloat HDPE modules:** square 50 × 50 cm blocks that click together in 50 cm steps, for floating platforms people can use (sitting, gathering, events).
- **More recycled hexagon modules:** the green hexagon ring keeps growing into seating pods and floating gardens that link the platforms together.

### Technical approach
- **Pontoon core:** concrete pontoons (e.g. Marinetek) for stability against ship wakes.
- **Add-ons:** Jetfloat HDPE modules (Austrian maker, since 1971; single element 50 × 50 × 40 cm) [src: Jetfloat catalogue], plus hexagon modules made from plastic caught by our trash stoppers (recycling partner: CLEAR RIVERS) [TBC with CLEAR RIVERS].
- **Green trash stopper:** planted hexagon modules plus a floating boom and catch basin (below).
- **Recycled hexagon modules:** linked hexagon modules made from recycled plastic. From day one we use recycled plastic from other sources (e.g. CLEAR RIVERS and PET Kupa collections); over time, the plastic our own trash stoppers collect is added. Proven in Rotterdam, where CLEAR RIVERS turned river plastic into 28 linked hexagon blocks. [src]

### How the trash stopper works
1. **Boom:** a floating barrier, a tube with a short skirt hanging below the surface, is anchored upstream of the pontoon at an angle to the current.
2. **Guiding:** the current pushes floating litter along the angled boom, away from the shipping lane and towards the pontoon.
3. **Catch basin:** at the end of the boom, litter enters a passive basin attached to the pontoon (like the CLEAR RIVERS trap at BME). It uses no energy; fish and water pass underneath.
4. **Green hexagon ring:** planted hexagon modules line the boom and the pontoon edge. Their roots and stems hold smaller pieces, give habitat and hide the boom, so it looks like a green island rather than a barrier.
5. **Emptying:** the club and volunteers empty the basin, e.g. weekly, sort the litter and record what they find. The data feeds the school workshops, and the plastic is recycled into our hexagon modules (Step 3).
6. **Floods and ships:** the boom only covers the bank side and has a quick release for high water, ice and ship traffic [TBC with the water authority].
- **Mooring:** piles or chains so everything rises and falls with the water level; HDPE add-ons can be removed for winter, ice and floods.
- **No swimming** in this version.

### Features
- **Core loop (Step 1):** clean bank → green pontoon → sports events and workshops → toilets → people come back and help care for it.
- **Engagement:** club membership, sports events, school workshops.
- **Later:** noise buffer, beach link, second pontoon, public calisthenics and yoga decks (Step 2); Jetfloat platforms and more hexagon modules (Step 3).

**Finding (the gap):** Budapest projects are either **swimming** (Árasztó), **commercial** (Flava), **temporary events on the Pest side** (RAKPART), or **slow city infrastructure** (BKK, 2029). Nobody makes a **permanent, green, community-run** riverside spot on the **Buda bank** that combines **floating greenery and trash catching, sports events, school education, toilets and noise calming**. Our main idea: **make the Danube beautiful and enjoyable so people take care of it.**

## 4. Target market

- **Who funds (customers):** sponsors (companies' CSR), the community (members, donors, volunteers), possibly Budapest / District XI / Hungary, and **BME as a partner that can invest in and help maintain the site**.
- **Who uses (users), everyone:** sports clubs and their events, local residents, families with children, school classes, students, visitors.
- **Out of scope:** swimming (for now), private or paid-entry use, motorised water sports.

### Core persona: sports organiser
- **Kata, 34, kayak and SUP club coach.**
- **Situation:** runs youth and adult training; launch points in the city centre are rare.
- **Goals:** a safe base with toilets to host regular events and attract new members.
- **Quote:** "Give me a clean place by the water and a toilet, and I'll bring 50 people every weekend."
- **Design consequences:** pontoon access for boats, event scheduling through the club, toilets, storage.

### Other personas
- **Anna, 38, mother of two, lives in District XI:** wants a safe, green spot by the river for weekend afternoons.
- **Gábor, 45, primary school teacher:** wants an organised river-awareness day for his class.
- **Mr. Szabó, 68, resident:** wants a calm, clean, quieter place to sit by the Danube.

### Reach (people, not euros)
- **TAM:** Budapest residents, ~1.7 million [src: general fact].
- **SAM:** people living or working within ~2 km of the site, ~100,000 [est].
- **SOM:** ~15,000–30,000 visits per season in Step 1 [est].

## 5. Business Model Canvas (non-profit)

- **Customer segments:** *funders:* sponsors, community, city/district, BME; *users:* sports clubs, residents, families, schools, students, visitors.
- **Value proposition:** a beautiful, clean, quieter riverside spot that is free and open, with sport and learning, so people reconnect with and care for the Danube.
- **Channels:** sports clubs, schools, District XI, local social media, BME, VALYO network.
- **Customer relationships:** club membership, volunteering, recurring events and workshops.
- **Funding streams (main):** sponsorship and the community (membership, donations, crowdfunding, volunteering). **Supporting:** public grants (incl. participatory budget), BME contribution, sports-club and school contributions.
- **Key resources:** existing pontoons, permits, community club, sports partners, modules and plants.
- **Key activities:** maintenance, emptying the trash catch, running events and workshops, toilets upkeep.
- **Key partners / stakeholders:** see section 13 (VALYO, CLEAR RIVERS, Stopper Úszóház / Kajak-Kenu Federation, Urban Rivers, PET Kupa, Audi Hungaria, District XI, City of Budapest, BME, MAHART, Jetfloat / Marinetek), plus sports clubs and schools.
- **Cost structure:** pontoon extension, wetlands, trash stopper, bank works, toilets, noise buffer, modules, insurance, materials. Upkeep is done by the community, not paid staff.

## 7. Porter's Five Forces (adapted for a non-profit)

- **Rivalry:** competition for sponsors and public attention with RAKPART, Árasztó and other green projects; medium.
- **New entrants:** low; permits and river works are a high barrier.
- **Substitutes:** parks, thermal baths, Margaret Island, RAKPART; medium.
- **Buyer (funder) power:** high; sponsors and the city decide the money and permits.
- **Supplier power:** medium; few pontoon and module suppliers.
- **Defence:** existing pontoons, community club, partnerships, first permanent green spot on this Buda stretch.

## 8. STEEPLE

- **Social:** people reconnect with the river; free and open; toilets.
- **Technological:** concrete pontoons, Jetfloat modules, recycled hexagon modules, floating wetlands, green noise barriers.
- **Economic:** low cost through reuse; community-run, no paid staff.
- **Environmental:** trash catching, habitat, greener bank; flood and ice risk.
- **Political:** the city is investing in Danube banks; permits involve several bodies.
- **Legal:** navigation and water-police rules; public-space permits; liability insurance.
- **Ethical:** must stay public and free, not a private club; accessible.

## 9. SWOT

- **Strengths:** reuses existing pontoons; step-by-step and low-risk; many natural partners.
- **Weaknesses:** depends on sponsors; seasonal use; permits take time.
- **Opportunities:** city's Danube agenda; EU river and climate funds; model can be repeated at other pontoons.
- **Threats:** floods, record low water, ship wakes, vandalism, sponsor drop-out.

## 10. Sustainability (3 pillars)

- **Environmental:** catches floating litter and turns it into new platform modules (circular), adds green habitat, cleaner bank, sustainable materials.
- **Social:** free public space, sports, school education, accessible toilets, community ownership.
- **Economic:** low cost through reuse; community-run upkeep; new blue activities for the public.
- **SDG and EU fit:** SDG 3, 6, 11, 14, 15; EU Mission "Restore our Ocean and Waters", Water Framework Directive.

## 11. Roadmap

- **Hackathon demo:** aerial concept image, pitch, this plan (Vienna, 24 Nov 2026).
- **Phase 0:** partners and permits (VALYO, CLEAR RIVERS, District XI, BME); choose the pilot pontoon; found the club.
- **Phase 1 (Step 1):** extend the first pontoon, green hexagon trash stopper, toilets, 20 m bank clean-up.
- **Phase 2 (Step 1):** first season: sports events and school workshops.
- **Phase 3 (Step 2):** see-through green noise buffer.
- **Phase 4 (Step 2):** beach link to the second pontoon; enlarge and green it; public calisthenics and yoga equipment on the pontoons.
